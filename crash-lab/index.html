anatomy of two crashes

trying to predict the next great market crash, in the open. v1 is an autopsy, not a forecast: what the classic sirens did before 2000 and 2008 — and 2020, for humility — on real data, with the misses left in.

// the tape

s&p 500, 1950 → now

monthly averages, log scale. the shaded bands are the three drawdowns this page keeps returning to. monthly averaging softens the spikes — the intraday lows were uglier.

// siren 1 · valuation

shiller cape

price over ten years of real earnings. it can't time anything — it just tells you how much optimism is already in the price when something does go wrong.

// siren 2 · fear

vix regime

option-implied volatility, chunk highs so the spikes survive. the bands are calm <15, normal, stressed >25, panic >40.

// backdrop · rates

the 10-year

long rates set the gravity that valuations argue against. the famous recession signal — the inverted yield curve — needs the short leg too, and I haven't found a free series I trust for it yet. so this is context, not a siren.

// what's missing

the sirens not on the wall yet

  • yield-curve inversion — the short leg (3m bill) has no clean free mirror I trust. on the bench.
  • margin debt — finra publishes it, but not in a form a static page can quote honestly. on the bench.
  • credit spreads — the 2008 siren. baa−aaa needs a source that won't rot. on the bench.
  • cape freshness — the free shiller mirror's earnings columns go stale; the cape line ends earlier than the price line. the gap is drawn, not hidden.
